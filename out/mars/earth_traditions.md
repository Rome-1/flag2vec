# Earth flag tradition geography

A 1-2 sentence note per vex category — where it dominates on Earth, and roughly when the form crystallized. Counts are over the 197 sovereign flags in `data/sovereign_flags.csv`, joined with `data/regions.csv`. This file is the reference Phase 4 uses when assigning each Mars region an inherited Earth tradition.

The question Phase 4 asks: if you carry these traditions to a freshly terraformed Mars and let DINOv2 embed the resulting flags, do the inheritance choices land near their Earth ancestors?

---

## Nordic cross  (n=5)

Dominates **Northern Europe** — all five Nordic states (Denmark, Sweden, Norway, Iceland, Finland) carry the offset cross, and nobody else does. Tightest visual cluster in DINOv2 (k=5 NN purity ≈ 0.64).
**Emerged:** **12th–13th c.** with the Dannebrog (legendary 1219, in continuous use since the 14th c.); Sweden's blue-and-yellow cross codified mid-1500s, the rest of the family settled by ~1900.

## Pan-Arab  (n=10)

Dominates **the Arab world** — 8 of 10 in Asia (Iraq, Jordan, Kuwait, Oman, Palestine, Syria, UAE, Yemen) plus Egypt and Sudan in North Africa. Red/white/black horizontal tricolor with green as the fourth color, often as a hoist triangle.
**Emerged:** **1916,** the flag of the Arab Revolt against the Ottomans; standardized in postcolonial republics through the 1950s–60s.

## Pan-African  (n=21)

Dominates **Sub-Saharan Africa** — by far the largest tradition in Africa (21/41 African flags). Two color families — Ethiopian (green/yellow/red, after Ethiopia's resistance to European colonization) and the Marcus Garvey UNIA palette (red/black/green) — applied to vertical or horizontal tricolors with optional central charges.
**Emerged:** Ethiopian colors **late 1890s** after the Battle of Adwa (1896); explicitly pan-African with **Ghana 1957,** then adopted en masse during decolonization 1960s–70s.

## Pan-Slavic  (n=8)

Dominates **Slavic Eastern Europe** — Russia, Serbia, Slovenia, Slovakia, Czech Republic, Croatia, Bulgaria, Poland (variants). Red/white/blue horizontal tricolor in various stripe orderings.
**Emerged:** **1830s–1840s** (Pan-Slav Congress, Prague 1848 codified red/white/blue as Slavic colors), tracing back to the Russian merchant ensign of **1668.** Resurged after 1989.

## British ensign  (n=5)

Dominates **British colonial successor states in Oceania** — Australia, New Zealand, Fiji, Tuvalu, plus the UK itself. Blue field with Union Jack in the canton. Tight cluster (~0.60 NN purity) despite different fly devices.
**Emerged:** **1801** (current Union Jack with the St. Patrick saltire added after Acts of Union); ensign convention systematized by the British Admiralty across the 19th c.

## Vertical tricolor  (n=12)

Spread between **Western Europe, francophone Africa, and the Americas.** France is the prototype; Italy, Belgium, Ireland, Romania, Moldova in Europe; Chad, Côte d'Ivoire, Nigeria in Africa; Mexico, Peru, St Vincent in the Americas.
**Emerged:** **1789–1794** with the French Revolution; weaponized by Napoleon's reorganization of Europe (Italy 1797, Belgium 1830, Ireland 1848); transferred via colonial influence to West Africa.

## Horizontal tricolor  (n=26)

The largest single tradition globally — **everywhere outside Latin America and the Anglosphere.** Especially dense in continental Europe (10) and Asia (7). The "neutral" form: any region without a stronger local convention tends to land here.
**Emerged:** Dutch **Prinsenvlag** ~**1572** (orange/white/blue, later red/white/blue); the Dutch ancestor seeded the Russian, German, Hungarian, and most other horizontal tricolors that crystallized 1789–1918.

## Star & crescent  (n=12)

Dominates **the Islamic world's non-Arab edges** — North Africa (Algeria, Tunisia, Libya, Mauritania, Comoros) and Turkic/Central Asia (Turkey, Azerbaijan, Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan, Pakistan, Maldives, Singapore). Note: not all Islamic states use it (the Arab states use pan-Arab instead).
**Emerged:** Late **Ottoman** era (~**1844** under Abdulmejid I as the empire's modern flag); inherited by Turkey 1923 and adopted across Ottoman successor states and Muslim states with Ottoman cultural ties.

## Communist red  (n=6)

Surviving in **East Asia + a few outliers** — China, Vietnam, North Korea, Mongolia, plus Belarus and Angola. Red field, often with a yellow star or socialist emblem. Almost zero clustering in DINOv2 because structure varies enormously beyond "red field."
**Emerged:** Soviet Union **1922** (red field with hammer-and-sickle); proliferated **1949–1975** through Asian and African communist revolutions, contracted sharply 1989–1991.

## Latin (charge)  (n=10)

Dominates **Spanish-speaking Latin America** — Argentina, Bolivia, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, Nicaragua, plus Haiti. Horizontal tricolor (often blue/white/blue or blue/white/red) with an elaborate central coat of arms.
**Emerged:** **1810s–1820s** during the Bolivarian wars of independence — Argentina (1812), Gran Colombia (1819–1830 dissolution split it into Colombia/Venezuela/Ecuador), Central American Federation (1823–1841 split into the five Central American flags). All trace through Manuel Belgrano and Francisco de Miranda.

## Saltire  (n=2)

A near-empty category on Earth — just **Burundi and Jamaica.** The diagonal cross dividing the flag into wedges. (Numerous subdivision flags use saltires: Scotland, Alabama, Florida, Northern Ireland.)
**Emerged:** Heraldic saltires go back to the **St Andrew's Cross of Scotland** (legendary 832, codified ~1385); modern national use is recent (Jamaica 1962, Burundi 1967).

## Solid + emblem  (n=18)

Wide distribution — **Asia (11) is the heartland** (Japan, South Korea, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Sri Lanka, Cyprus, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Afghanistan, Laos, Vietnam-style), with secondary nodes in Europe (Albania, North Macedonia), Africa (Morocco, Somalia), Oceania, and the Americas (Canada). Single solid background, single distinctive central charge.
**Emerged:** **Hinomaru** (Japan) traced to the **12th c.,** codified 1870; the modern "solid + central charge" form is heavily Asian and proliferates with **20th-c. independence** (Bangladesh 1972, Sri Lanka 1948, Cyprus 1960, Canada 1965).

## Stars & stripes  (n=9)

The **United States** plus a scatter of US-influenced or US-derived flags (Liberia, Cuba, Chile, Uruguay, Malaysia, Taiwan, Greece, Samoa). Multiple horizontal stripes with a canton in the upper hoist.
**Emerged:** **1777** (US Flag Resolution); Liberia 1847 most directly derivative; Greece 1822 independently developed similar stripes; Malaysia and Cuba late-19th/20th c. with explicit US influence.

## Heraldic  (n=12)

Dominates **Western/Southern Europe's old monarchies** — Spain, Portugal, Andorra, San Marino, Vatican, Liechtenstein, Malta, Montenegro plus Belize, Brunei, Sri Lanka, Eswatini. Coat-of-arms-on-field designs that predate the modern tricolor era.
**Emerged:** Late **medieval / early modern** (~**12th–17th c.**); these are the survivors of the pre-1789 European flag landscape that the French Revolution and its imitators didn't sweep away.

## Unique  (n=41)

The catch-all for flags that don't slot into any tradition — Switzerland, Israel, Nepal, Brazil, Guyana, Kiribati, Vanuatu, Bhutan, Botswana, the South African post-1994 Y-flag, etc. Largest residual bucket; primarily flags where a strong **post-independence design choice** explicitly broke from a regional template.
**Emerged:** Mostly **20th c. independence** (Brazil 1889, Israel 1948, post-1960s African and Pacific states), with a few much older outliers (Switzerland adopted 1889 but the white cross dates to ~1300, Nepal's double-pennon to the 19th c.).

## Notes for Phase 4 inheritance

- **Climate-coded** traditions to lean on: Nordic cross (Northern, frozen), Pan-African (warm/agricultural), Star & crescent (desert).
- **Form-coded** (not climate-coded): tricolors, heraldic, saltire — assignable on geological/political analogy rather than climate.
- **Politics-coded:** Communist red (commemorative), British ensign (settler-colonial), Stars & stripes (broad agricultural plain + federal structure), Latin charge (post-independence republican).
- **Pan-Arab and Pan-Slavic** are loose enough that "broad continental tradition with shared color tradition" is the analogy — useful for Mars regions named after Earth toponyms (Arabia Terra, Terra Sirenum, Sabaea).
