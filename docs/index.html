five things DINOv2 saw

01

Nordic crosses are visual twins.

Sweden, Denmark, Norway, Iceland, and Finland sit in the tightest cluster of any tradition — 64% k=5 NN purity in 384-dim space. The structure is the message: a single asymmetric cross, even when the colors vary.

02

British ensigns crossed an ocean and stayed siblings.

The UK, Australia, New Zealand, Fiji, and Tuvalu share a canton-on-blue template — and despite very different fly designs, DINOv2 places them together. Australian state flags pile in too (31× k-NN lift).

03

Latin America beats geography.

Region matters less than tradition: European flags are scattered everywhere, but Latin American horizontal-tricolour-with-charge forms a tight visual subcluster spanning two continents. Argentina, Uruguay, Honduras, El Salvador — visual cousins, political coincidence.

04

Communist red doesn't cluster.

China, Vietnam, North Korea, Belarus, Mongolia, Angola — share a colour, not a structure. DINOv2 sees beyond palette to layout, so a red field with a star, a hammer-and-compass, an ornate seal, and a black stripe scatter across the map.

05

Mars inherits — sometimes.

Twenty-five Martian regional flags, each procedurally derived from an Earth tradition. 13 of 25 land closest to their inherited centroid in DINOv2 space. Structurally distinctive traditions (Nordic, solid+emblem, pan-Arab) inherit cleanly; color-only traditions (heraldic, pan-African) scatter.
